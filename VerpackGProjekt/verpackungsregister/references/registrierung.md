Companies that introduce and sell their packaged goods on the German market on a commercial basis have to fulfil packaging law obligations. These apply to companies from Germany and abroad. This knowledge base provides importers with an overview of all the information they need to fulfil their obligations.

'Import' in this case means the cross-border trading/purchase of goods from abroad (including other EU Member States) and their introduction to/sale in the German market.

The party bearing legal responsibility for the packaged goods at the time they cross the border is the party under obligation under packaging law and is considered the importer of the goods. Legal responsibility means that the party introducing the goods to the German market bears the transport risk for loss of or damage to the goods, for example, and is responsible for customs clearance. The question of who bears legal responsibility should be contractually agreed upon between the parties involved before the goods are placed on the German market.

Heads-up: Incoterms, international commercial terms that are used to determine the contractual rights and obligations of each contracting party, can serve as a point of reference.

The specific point in time that the transfer of ownership occurs is not a decisive factor. An appointed external freight forwarder / carrier is not considered to be a company placing the packaging on the German market for the first time; it is instead the ordering party.

Regardless of what type of packaging importers use to introduce their goods commercially for the first time to the German market – they must always register with the LUCID Packaging Register. The specific obligations depend on the packaging: if it typically accumulates as waste with private final consumers, you need to pay for its recycling.

No matter the type of packaging – if you place goods on the German market, you must register with the LUCID Packaging Register. The registration requirement also applies to companies that place low packaging volumes on the market.

What types of packaging are there? What are the statutory obligations that come with each of them?

Registration with the LUCID Packaging Register is free and is done online. To register, you must first create a login as a 'producer'. Producers include all kinds of manufacturers of packaged goods, distributors and importers as well as mail order companies and online retailers. Usually, they are the party filling the packaging.

When registering with the LUCID Packaging Register, you are required to enter your company name, the designated person (e.g. the CEO or managing director) and the specific contact person in the company, including their respective e-mail address. You will then receive an e-mail with an activation link that must be accessed within 24 hours.

Once you have created your login, you have seven days to complete your registration: enter your master data, add packaging types and brand names, and submit your application for registration.

Upon completion of registration, the ZSVR will send you an e-mail with your registration number. You will also automatically receive an administrative act by electronic means to officially confirm that you have successfully completed your registration. From that point onwards, you will be visible in the LUCID Packaging Register with your registration number, brand names and packaging details. The LUCID Packaging Register is public.

The following data is required:

Company address including telephone number

VAT number or taxpayer reference number

National identification number (such as commercial register number, business notification/registration or trade membership number)

Packaging types used by the company to place its goods on the German market

Brand names under which the company distributes its packaged goods

The ZSVR's LUCID Packaging Register is a public register that includes industrial and retail companies from Germany and abroad that have to fulfil obligations under German packaging law. Its objective is to create a fair market and establish a level playing field by instilling transparency. Publication enables everyone to search through the register and to check who has registered. That makes it easier to detect breaches of the Verpackungsgesetz (Packaging Act).
